Trump Says U.S. Will Lift 10% Tariff on Irish Whiskey

President Donald Trump said Sunday that the United States will remove its current 10% tariff on Irish whiskey after repeated requests from Irish officials and industry advocates.

By Rally Point News Desk · Published September 13, 2026 · Multi-source verified synthesis

Reuters and the Associated Press both reported that Trump announced the change during a visit to Ireland, saying he had decided to remove the tariff after being pressed on the issue. The announcement came during the Irish Open at Trump International Golf Links in Doonbeg.

What is established

The current U.S. tariff on Irish whiskey is 10%. Both Reuters and AP reported that the rate had previously been higher and was reduced earlier this year as the administration adjusted broader tariff policy affecting European Union imports. Trump said Sunday that Irish whiskey would now be exempted from that tariff.

The move was welcomed by industry groups. Reuters reported that the Irish Whiskey Association supported the announcement and renewed its call for zero-for-zero tariff treatment for alcoholic beverages traded between the United States and Europe. AP reported that the Distilled Spirits Council of the United States also praised the decision, arguing that lower duties would help U.S. hospitality businesses and spirits companies.

What remains uncertain

The announcement did not immediately provide the administrative details needed to show exactly when the tariff removal will take effect. AP reported that no implementation timeline was specified. Until the White House, U.S. Customs and Border Protection, or another relevant federal authority publishes the formal change, the announcement is best understood as a stated policy decision whose execution details remain pending.

Why it matters

The United States is a major market for Irish whiskey, so even a relatively modest tariff affects importers, distributors, bars, retailers, and producers on both sides of the Atlantic. The decision also matters because it marks another adjustment to a tariff regime that has changed repeatedly during 2026. For businesses, the practical significance will depend on the formal effective date and the precise products covered.

Editorial note: Rally Point News is distinguishing the president's announced policy decision from its formal implementation. This brief will treat the tariff as removed only once the applicable government action takes effect.

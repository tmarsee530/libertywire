Tom Tiffany Plane Makes Emergency Landing in Lake Wausau

Rep. Tom Tiffany and the pilot of a small plane escaped with minor injuries after the aircraft lost power while approaching Wausau Downtown Airport and made an emergency water landing Saturday night.

By Rally Point News Desk · Published September 13, 2026 · 3-source verified synthesis

Multiple reports agree on the central sequence: the aircraft lost power while approaching Wausau Downtown Airport, the pilot put the plane down in Lake Wausau, and Tiffany and the pilot got out as the aircraft began to submerge. They moved into shallow water and were rescued by emergency responders.

Reuters reported that Tiffany said he and the pilot were taken to Aspirus Wausau Hospital and treated for minor injuries. The Associated Press reported that Tiffany sustained a cut above his right eye that required stitches. Wisconsin Public Radio, citing the Marathon County Sheriff’s Office, reported that the 911 call came at 8:49 p.m. Saturday and that responders reached the two men by airboat at about 9:05 p.m.

What is established

The flight was returning from the La Crosse County Lincoln Day Dinner when the loss of power occurred on approach to Wausau. The pilot made the emergency water landing after determining the aircraft would not reach the runway, according to information reported by Wisconsin Public Radio from local authorities. Tiffany and the pilot were the only people aboard, and both were released after treatment for minor injuries.

What remains uncertain

The reason the aircraft lost power has not been established publicly. The Associated Press reported that the Federal Aviation Administration will investigate and that the National Transportation Safety Board could become involved depending on the assessment of the aircraft’s damage. Until investigators release findings, the mechanical cause should be treated as unresolved.

Why it matters

The event is significant primarily as an aviation emergency involving a sitting member of Congress and a current Wisconsin gubernatorial candidate. The immediate public-safety facts are comparatively clear: both occupants survived, injuries were minor, and the aircraft went into the lake after a reported loss of power. The next factual development is likely to come from aviation investigators rather than political reporting.

Editorial note: Rally Point News is treating the cause of the power loss as unknown unless and until investigators publish findings.

Why Did Amazon Suspend 21 Air After the Miami Cargo Plane Crash?

Amazon says it paused operations with cargo carrier 21 Air after reviewing circumstances surrounding the fatal crash of Flight 7598. Federal investigators have released preliminary flight-recorder information, but they have not determined the crash’s probable cause.

By Rally Point News Desk · Published September 14, 2026 · Multi-source verified synthesis

Amazon has suspended operations with 21 Air, the cargo airline that operated Amazon Air Flight 7598 when the Boeing 767 overran Runway 30 at Miami International Airport on September 6. Five people on the ground were killed and five others were injured, according to the Associated Press. The two pilots survived.

Amazon announced the pause on September 13, saying it made the decision after supporting the investigation and reviewing “some of the surrounding circumstances.” The company did not publicly identify a single finding as the reason for the suspension. Reuters and AP independently confirmed the operational pause.

What has the NTSB established about Flight 7598?

The National Transportation Safety Board says Flight 7598 was a Part 121 cargo flight from San Juan, Puerto Rico, to Miami. The aircraft overran the end of Runway 30 during landing.

In a September 9 investigative update, the NTSB said both the cockpit voice recorder and flight data recorder had been recovered and successfully read. The agency emphasized that the information released so far is preliminary and subject to change.

Reuters reported that recorder data showed the pilots considered aborting the landing after touchdown and that one pilot warned the aircraft was “too fast” shortly before touchdown. AP similarly reported cockpit warnings about speed and descent. Those facts are investigative evidence, not a final determination of why the crash occurred.

Why did Amazon stop using 21 Air?

Amazon’s public explanation is limited. In its own update, the company said safety is a priority and that, after reviewing circumstances surrounding the crash, it decided to pause operations with 21 Air. Amazon said it would continue supporting the investigation and people affected by the crash.

The suspension therefore reflects Amazon’s operational decision while the investigation continues; it should not be read as a federal finding that 21 Air caused the crash or violated a specific safety rule.

What is still unknown?

The NTSB has not announced a probable cause. Investigators are expected to examine multiple factors, including flight-crew actions, aircraft performance and maintenance, weather, operating procedures and other evidence collected during the investigation.

A final NTSB probable-cause determination typically comes well after the initial fact-finding stage. Until then, preliminary recorder information can establish the sequence of events without establishing ultimate responsibility.

What changes for Amazon Air?

The immediate change is that Amazon is no longer using 21 Air for its cargo operations while the pause remains in place. Amazon has not described the suspension as permanent. Reuters reported that 21 Air said it remained focused on supporting the families affected and cooperating with the NTSB while expressing confidence in its safety policies, procedures and training.

What remains unresolved: The cause of the September 6 crash has not been determined. Amazon has not said how long its pause with 21 Air will last, and the NTSB’s preliminary information should not be treated as a final finding of fault.
